The Youngest

We have always known who you are. We simply declined to tell you ahead of time. This is traditional.

Who You Are

The youngest person at the seder has asked the Four Questions since at least the second century CE. You are part of a chain of youngest people stretching back through every seder ever held, in every country Jews have lived in, in every language they've spoken. When you ask ma nishtana, you are asking it alongside all of them.

Your One Job

מַה נִּשְׁתַּנָּה הַלַּיְלָה הַזֶּה מִכָּל הַלֵּילוֹת
Ma nishtana halaila hazeh mikol haleilot?

Why is this night different from all other nights?

  1. On all other nights: bread or matzah. Tonight: only matzah.
  2. On all other nights: all vegetables. Tonight: bitter herbs.
  3. On all other nights: no dipping. Tonight: we dip twice.
  4. On all other nights: sitting or reclining. Tonight: we all recline.

What Comes Next

God explains the reclining — it's ironic, given the circumstances. You're released from duty after that, unless you find the Afikomen, in which case you hold it for ransom. No demand is too unreasonable.

The Bigger Picture

The question "why is this night different" is not just about matzah. It is asked so that the answer can be given — so that someone has to explain, out loud, what happened and why it matters. You are the reason we tell the story tonight. That's not nothing.
